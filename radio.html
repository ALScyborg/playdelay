Stations

Team radio PlayDelay actually plays

Each row is a team in the PlayDelay player. The station is the stream the player opens, taken from the same team list the app uses. For most schools that is the network flagship, not every affiliate. The player opens that one stream for football and for men’s basketball. To line the call up with a television picture, use the sync guide.

The play-by-play column is narrower on purpose. A name is listed only when a 2026 athletics page, official game note, flagship-station page, or game-day broadcast listing named that person. A dash means we did not confirm a name. It does not mean the team has no announcer. Crews change, and a basketball broadcast is sometimes a different voice than football. Where a source named both, both are in the cell. This list was checked on November 18, 2026.

How to read a few of the rows

Kansas State is the exception in the player. The schedule loads, and Play is not available, because no stream is connected. Kansas State Athletics’ 2026 football notes list the network flagships as KMAN (1350 AM, and 93.3 and 93.7 FM) and KMKF (101.5 FM), with Wyatt Thompson on the football call, Stan Weber as analyst, and Matt Walters on the sideline. That is the public network. It is not a stream inside PlayDelay yet.

Cincinnati in the player is NewsRadio 700 WLW. Cincinnati Athletics’ 2026 football crew announcement puts Dan Hoard on play-by-play for most dates, with Mo Egger listed for the November 7 game at Houston. Some September kickoffs also air on 102.7 WEBN, and WLW may leave the game for Reds baseball. If the stream you hear is not the football call, that split is why.

Arizona State is Arizona Sports 98.7. Game-day coverage can be blacked out outside Phoenix. The player uses the station’s continuous mount, which may still play when the game-day feed does not. Tim Healey is the voice of Sun Devil football and men’s basketball in Arizona State’s 2026 radio listing.

USC is ESPN LA 710. USC Athletics’ 2026 game notes put Pete Arbogast on the English game-day broadcast, including the pregame and postgame shows, and they do not label which seat on that English broadcast is play-by-play. The Spanish broadcast is labeled: Harry Ruiz calls the game. The player uses the station’s stable live stream, not a one-off session link.

Nebraska is 1400 & 99.3 KLIN. Kyle Crooks is the football play-by-play voice on the Huskers Radio Network. Men’s basketball on that network is Kent Pavelka. The player follows the same stream redirect pattern it uses for Arizona.

BYU is KSL NewsRadio, Greg Wrubell on football and men’s basketball. Utah is ESPN 700 KALL, Bill Riley on both. LSU is Eagle 98.1 WDGL, Chris Blair on football and men’s basketball. Those three are the clearest “one voice, both sports” cases in the schools’ own staff pages.

Schools added on November 18, 2026 use the same rule: the station in the table is a public stream of the football broadcast that actually returned audio. A few do not match one flagship for every game. Indiana’s Bloomington flagship, WHCC 105.1, does not publish a browser stream, so the player uses 93.1 WIBC in Indianapolis, which carries the full football broadcast. Boston College’s late-season games are on WEEI 93.7; some early 2026 dates were on 850 WEEI only. Florida State men’s basketball is on 100.7 WFLA, not 94.9 TNT. SMU men’s basketball is not on The Ticket. Stanford’s 2026 men’s basketball notes do not list a terrestrial flagship. Missouri’s KTGR page says game audio on the station stream may be limited to within 75 miles of Columbia. Ole Miss is not in the player. Its 2026 Oxford home station, WOXF 105.1, has no public stream the player can open.

Colorado, Texas Tech, Miami, and Notre Dame are in the player with the stations shown above. No 2026 play-by-play name is listed for them here, because we did not confirm one. If you know the station is wrong, or you want a school that is not in this list, write support@playdelay.app and name the station you listen to. There is no request form in the app.
