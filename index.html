The radio call, lined up with the picture

College football and men’s basketball on television run behind the local radio broadcast of the same game. The network picture is encoded, then buffered again by a cable box, a satellite receiver, or a streaming app such as YouTube TV or Hulu. The flagship radio station is much closer to live. Leave both unmuted and the radio announcer calls a tackle you have not seen yet. The announcer is not early. The picture is late.

PlayDelay plays that flagship stream in the browser and holds it for a delay you set, from live up to two minutes. You mute the television, keep the picture, and hear the station you wanted. Presets start at 5, 8, 10, and 15 seconds. One-second nudges finish the match. The same number usually works next week on the same screen. A streaming app often needs longer than a cable box, because the app is holding video so the picture does not stall. The sync guide is the full walk-through, including what to do when the delay engine cannot run and the player falls back to live audio.

How it works

  1. Watch on TV

    Put the game on your television and mute the TV audio.

  2. Pick your team

    Open the player, tap a team, and play the flagship radio call. Kansas State’s stream is still coming soon. The station list names every flagship.

  3. Dial the delay

    Use presets or ± nudges until the radio matches what you see.
